# Pack One Lab
Tracking: [#1791](https://github.com/killjoy00/mtg-ev-analyzer/issues/1791).

## Charter
**Exploring how we draft Magic.** The reader need not know Pack One. Lab is a public Limited research feature, not product reporting. Model/score/difficulty/puzzle selection findings belong in Inside Pack One. The three editorial series are Draft Theory, Format Deep Dive, and The Drafting Divide.

Use the public-facing Lab plain-language style rules in `writing-style.mjs`. Use a natural independent editorial voice, never authorial we/our/us. Draft Note, Draft Theory and Format Deep Dive are flexible editorial directions, not required templates. Named-card drafts may be one short paragraph, with no minimum number of headings or closing lesson. Bounds are 12–175, 12–310 and 12–330 prose words respectively. Lead with the concrete card and finding, not an invented opening. Legacy offline packets retain their older assembly contracts. No forced discussion question or boilerplate conclusion. The word ranges exclude deterministic numeric evidence and source note. Prefer specific cards and packs only when validated, otherwise use an explicitly hypothetical situation. Avoid generic advice, excessive promotion, and claims of a correct pick or causal advantage from observational data. Include source/license, actual observation unit, cohort, date window/archival provenance, sample size, missingness and relevant caveats.

## Current architecture: retained-data first

The regular Lab schedule reads the already approved, versioned PremierDraft trophy corpus in Neon and the existing checksum-verified last-game trophy deck catalogs. It never downloads a 17Lands archive, and does not require a separate data ingestion process. Normal Pack One new-set ingestion makes newly approved Live sets available automatically. The worker chooses the five most recently released regular Live sets, with a ranking preference for the newest three, reads only the active approved source snapshot for each, and calculates a private weekly aggregate report inside a repeatable-read snapshot. Previous model/corpus versions, unapproved components, excluded sources and moderated removals are not pooled. No Pack One player/account records, model probabilities, draft IDs/links or individual pack records are sent to the AI writer; only aggregate evidence, observed public card names/type/rarity/mana metadata, and clearly labeled hypothetical examples.

Migration 0083 creates two read-only projected research views, separate from the queue tables. They include the minimum public-source fields needed for per-draft opportunity counts and exact deck-catalog matching. Grant the dedicated worker SELECT only on these views, never the underlying corpus/player tables. Revoke PUBLIC access. Only valid observed early-pick histories, known win-rate buckets (at least 60%, at least 100 prior games), and seven-win verified trophy sources are eligible. This is the retained cohort, not a claim to cover all 17Lands drafts. Each set's report includes the active source snapshot, archived SHA-256 pins, and matched deck catalog hash. Source keys identify set and topic; repeated topics for a set are suppressed. The cache refreshes weekly, so a newly launched set is included on the next preparation cycle.

Eight retained-data analyses, plus an explicitly defined newest-three-set opening creature comparison: repeated nonland card names at P1P5, creature choices at P1P1/P1P5, common vs rare/mythic choices, early mono-color pivots, generic-mana spell choices, two-drop creature choices, opening card-name inclusion in the last recorded main deck versus sideboard, and recorded last-deck splash rate. Sample floors, metadata/observation coverage, exact stage comparisons, frozen evidence and descriptive caveats still apply. These deck findings are **not** first-game or game-weighted measures. Missing catalogs/metadata or insufficient opportunities suppress the affected finding. The independent historical raw-archive adapter below is optional offline research, not a schedule or merge requirement.

Read-only production spot check (November 24, 2026): Reality Fracture retained 1,029 complete eligible first-eight trajectories after exclusions in the targeted check. When both a common nonland and rare/mythic nonland were offered, common nonland picks were 104/939 at P1P1 (11.1%) and 139/301 at P1P5 (46.2%). The denominators are different conditional opportunities, not evidence of a causal shift or a better pick. This direct SQL cross-check is not an assertion that the entire scheduled worker has executed on production.

## Phase 1: offline proof of concept

- `pilot.mjs`: two deterministic historical analyses using the existing pinned public FRA aggregate (not fresh database data). Validates source hashes, counts, sample floor and consistency; constructs evidence packets, fixed-template draft copy, and SVG chart from the same computed numbers. The source is read-only. No AI or external service is used in this prototype.
- Splashing: 13,513 / 28,424 **games** with recorded splash colors (47.5%). Do not reinterpret as the percentage of unique decks, drafters, winning drafts, or optimal picks.
- Duplicate copies: 3,984 / 21,545 **draft/card-name groups** with repeated picks and at least one observed deck copy had fewer observed deck copies than drafted (18.5%). Maximum counts across recorded games are used; coverage may be incomplete. Do not claim the extra picks were mistakes. The wider repeated-card population is 26,258 groups and must not be mixed into that conditional denominator.
- Both are descriptive observations from one archived set, without independent-draft confidence intervals. They are eligible for editorial review, not release. The numbers of repeated games/card groups do not establish independent sample sizes.
- Pilot source: [FRA audit](../CONTEXT-MODEL-AUDIT.md), [aggregate JSON](../data/fra-context-audit.json), [17Lands public datasets](https://www.17lands.com/public_datasets), CC BY 4.0. The CLI and tests pin the original Git blob; future sources require explicit reviewed version updates.

## Phase 1b: actual trophy-pick archive adapter and editorial triage

The offline Python adapter `scripts/pack_one_lab_picks.py` reuses the existing qualified-trophy cohort parser, complete first-eight trajectory validation, first-game deck join and explicit mana-cost classifier. It accepts only the three pinned FRA archive/metadata SHA-256 inputs from the existing context audit. It produces aggregate per-pick evidence, not raw draft IDs, player records, article drafts or source packs. Requires an existing private output directory outside the repo and refuses Actions. Run on a trusted private worker, not as a manual owner requirement.

Five analysis implementations are present:
- fixing lands (known multicolor-producing land, not every form of fixing);
- off-color mono-color choices after at least two prior ordinary colored spells in one color (not proof of pivoting);
- generic-mana spells versus colored alternatives (not complete flexibility);
- common vs rare/mythic spells (blocked for the current pinned rules snapshot because it lacks rarity);
- first-pick card-name inclusion in the first recorded match/game deck (not copy-level or final-deck inclusion).

Per-pick sample gates are 100 distinct qualifying source drafts and 80% metadata/observation coverage. No counts below the gate enter the findings. These are initial descriptive editorial thresholds, not significance tests. Cross-pick cohorts may overlap and the offered alternatives differ. No causal comparisons. Synthetic test cases establish intended mechanics, not Magic findings. No real archive run has been verified yet.

For a private worker with the exact pinned inputs:
```sh
python scripts/pack_one_lab_picks.py --draft /private/draft.csv.gz --game /private/games.csv.gz --metadata /private/fra-metadata.json.gz --out /private/lab/evidence.json
node scripts/pack-one-lab-triage.mjs --input /private/lab/evidence.json --out-dir /private/lab --exclude early-fixing
```
Omit the optional exclusion list if no questions have already been covered. The triage stage validates provenance fields, denominators, labels, coverage, stages and sums. It chooses predefined P1P1/P1P5 or P1P3/P1P6 comparisons, not whichever post-hoc comparison looks most exciting. Initial heuristic: require a five-percentage-point descriptive signal, prefer magnitude, sample size and coverage; suppress repeated topics, keep one candidate per theme and at most three briefs. A skip-week result is valid. Each brief has an explicitly hypothetical opening, exact observations, chart-ready data, discussion prompt, source and required limitations. A brief is not a finished article and has no publishing permissions. The triage does not independently re-read the archives; an independent review and source execution are still necessary.

Tests (synthetic fixtures only for the new adapter and triage):
```sh
python -m unittest discover -s tests -p test_pack_one_lab_picks.py
node --test tests/pack-one-lab-editorial.test.mjs
```

## Phase 2a: scheduled private approval pilot (implementation, not enabled)

The approved editorial cadence is Central time:
- Tuesday morning: calculate and privately cache the current retained corpus report; retry failed preparation on a subsequent scheduled tick. No external archive request.
- Wednesday morning: generate an article using an independently configured writer and reviewer; notify the owner at or after 9 AM when the article is ready.
- Wednesday–Friday: re-run on hourly ticks to process revision or alternative requests and notify the owner of the new revision.
- Friday noon: requested publication time. **No publisher is connected in this PR.** An approval only records the exact version and intended time; never implies a public post. No action means no post.

`worker/lab-writer.mjs` builds the article from a constrained AI response plus deterministic evidence, exact numerator/denominator, limitations, chart data, and source. Numeric claims and URLs are not accepted from AI prose. A separately configured reviewer must pass the result within three attempts. This is a provisional editorial gate, not proof every qualitative claim is correct. The worker uses a 20-point penalty for repeating the last article's form or theme; a strong finding may still win. The weekly runner uses only qualified retained-corpus findings from the rolling five-set window. The two historical aggregate prototypes remain available for offline research, but are not scheduled fallback candidates. Older sets may eventually be used as comparison context for a recent-set lead, never as standalone scheduled topics. If the recent window has no qualifying new finding, skip rather than reach back. Already-attempted set/topic pairs do not recur across weeks; when qualified evidence is exhausted, the pilot skips. It never downloads raw archives. The ten-article historical pilot remains a prerequisite for routine public publication.

`worker/lab-editorial.mjs` uses the ordinary authenticated owner membership and explicit gateway route allowlist. The queue stores a canonical hash of article body/title/chart/source and increments revisions. An atomic state/revision/hash compare-and-set and owner role recheck bind approval to that precise revision. An edit request or alternative never retains approval. The UI under Publishing → Pack One Lab renders text without interpreting AI HTML, shows the chart values, and supports review, revision, alternative, and skip. Do not enter credentials or personal data in feedback; the editorial worker forwards it to the AI provider. Approved revisions cannot be regenerated. The same SQL transaction records each owner decision. Old weeks cannot be approved. No article content is logged or stored in a public artifact.

`scripts/pack-one-lab-weekly.mjs` implements the queue state machine, timezone gating, exclusive database session advisory lock, deduplication, and private notification. Resend notification uses a per-revision claim and idempotency key, with a fixed operator address already used by Pack One launch alerts. A provider error or ambiguous send is not retried automatically; reconcile the private provider receipt and queue state, never clear a claim blindly. Provider acceptance is not proof of inbox receipt. Notification sends only an authenticated admin link, not article content or approval credentials.

## Editorial research direction

Prioritize interesting, format-specific findings over article length. Card-level topics should include named commons chosen when a rare or mythic is available, changes in selection rate after a prior copy, and which specific noncreature spells account for opening-pick patterns. Each lead must retain the exact opportunity definition and denominators. Compare the same card at the same stage where feasible; differences in lane, pack composition, earlier choices and player selection are confounders, not demonstrated causes. Do not call a selection rate a win-rate effect. Use additional verified examples only when they genuinely add substance. A brief finding can be sufficient; skip predictable or generic articles. The five named-card research families are now implemented in card-research.mjs. The weekly candidate pool is restricted to these families for the editorial pilot; generic category findings remain available for offline analysis and regression tests, never automatic fallback. A compelling single short finding is sufficient. If no eligible named-card lead survives history and independent editorial review, skip the week.

### Named-card research contract (v6 weekly cache)

- Accumulate one name-level opportunity per actual pick, deduplicating multiple printings of the same name. Flag conflicting metadata. No individual draft IDs, raw picks, model probabilities, player data, or private vote histories are in the persisted aggregate report. Retain actual counts and corpus fingerprints; source identities stay inside the private aggregation process.
- Five predefined families: common versus offered rare/mythic, same-card P1P1/P1P5 changes with a same-rarity card-offer baseline, prior-copy associations across P1P4–P1P8 with distinct-draft support and a fixed-stage follow-up when supported, named noncreature opening-pick rates and their share of all such picks, and top-five noncreature concentration across the newest three qualified sets. All use exact opportunity denominators and minimum support/coverage. No invented oracle text; metadata provides name, printed type category, rarity and mana cost, not abilities.
- The fixed-stage check chooses the largest joint support, not the biggest observed difference. It does not control color lane, alternative quality or prior preferences. Named rare co-offer examples are small descriptive samples, not head-to-head comparisons. The baseline is pooled over card-name offers (multiple per pack), not independent draft observations. These exploratory screens are not confirmatory statistical tests and cannot establish causation, optimal picks or win-rate effects.
- Deterministic ranking caps the effect contribution, rewards sample/coverage and useful supporting evidence, favors the newest three sets, and retains the previous form/theme diversity penalties. The writer must use an actual lead card in prose; the reviewer rejects generic restatement. Deterministic additional counts and method/source notes are bound into the exact approval hash. Do not send weak or generic substitutes merely to maintain cadence.
- Read-only live validation executed the actual named-card accumulator on projected retained trajectories for the five latest live regular sets, with source hashes replaced by anonymous group keys in memory. Results: 1,029 FRA, 1,119 HOB, 2,090 MSH, and 3,206 SOS complete first-eight sources; TMT had zero complete eligible first-eight sources in the queried snapshot, so it generated no lead. Seventeen candidate packets qualified; no data or production configuration was changed. HOB Lakeshore Apothecary matched independent aggregate results: 111/465 fresh offers vs 35/45 prior-copy offers; P1P7 check 23/75 vs 11/12. HOB rare co-offer leader Crude Bent Blade 56/169; MSH Web Up 61/212. Three-set top-five noncreature concentration: FRA 99/428, HOB 187/421, MSH 198/612. These are verified descriptive research leads, not live AI-generated or published posts. Full native publication and historical backtesting remain pending.

Optional card-strength context uses the committed public `corpus/card-strength/<set>.json` tables only when the game archive SHA-256 exactly matches the active source. It records the table fingerprint, requires at least 400 in-hand games for each included card, and presents the existing 400-pseudo-game-shrunk percentage-point difference versus the set mean. These are all-archive observations, not the retained trophy-pick sample, and must never be interpreted as causal improvements. Missing or mismatched tables simply omit the context. This is a read-only reuse of existing catalog data; no new archive downloads, scoring changes or provider calls.

For near-unanimous opening spells (at least 40 offers, at least 90% selection, at most 10 total other picks), preserve the exact other selected public card names, aggregate counts and nonconflicting rarity. The concentration comparison also removes the leading name from both numerator and denominator to check whether the remaining four leaders still account for a large share. Both checks are descriptive. Named exception counts are not evidence that a specific alternative is better.

### Plain-language editorial gate

The shared `writing-style.mjs` rules are applied to both the writer and independent reviewer; the deterministic assembly rejects selected stock transitions and hype, generic headings, formulaic contrast, em dashes in generated prose, and more than one body question in a named-card story. Exact supplied public card names are exempt from wording checks. The rule set contains only public-facing editorial instructions, not private writing samples. Named-card prose can use an exact supplied `[stat:<metric-id>:n|d|pct]` token in a normal sentence; the assembler verifies and substitutes counts/rates directly from the packet, rejects unknown or direct model-written numbers, and retains all chart metrics and source evidence in the approval record. The reviewer still checks the meaning and denominator of each claim. In-line numbers are not duplicated as a separate focus-metric sentence; the chart remains accessible and exact. The `style_version` is included in the immutable article hash. A short draft may use no extra sections or closing. No new publishing, live AI call, or production change.

### Follow-up investigations: copy counts (v7 weekly cache)

The ordinary candidate ingestion runner derives copy-count outcomes from its already-downloaded game archive before deleting the raw inputs. The optional aggregate is stored separately from the immutable puzzle/ledger manifest. Its exact SHA is in the reviewed promotion catalog and the loader validates it before writes; new sets use no extra downloads. The protected loader stores the exact report bytes in `pack1_lab_copy_outcomes`, keyed by set, game archive SHA and definition. Grant the private Lab worker SELECT only on this table alongside the existing two read-only corpus views; never grant serving or player-table access. The aggregate is not used by scoring or serving. Missing coverage is nonfatal and cannot be presented as game evidence.

The full-game archive includes non-trophy outcomes; valid game identity is deduplicated (all duplicate rows excluded), exact 1/2/3/4+ maindeck counts and pooled 2+ counted independently, with wins, games and distinct draft counts. Require at least 150 games and 30 distinct drafts in each reported cell. The largest shared-support exact two-main-color/prior-skill stratum is chosen before looking at outcome direction (>=100 prior games; <50%, 50–59%, >=60% prior win-rate buckets). Repeated game outcomes are clustered within drafts; no significance or causal claim is made. Same color/skill does not fully adjust deck quality, player selection or other confounders. Use the follow-up to support, challenge or qualify the pick finding, never to invent card text. Missing or mismatched evidence must not yield a win-rate claim. Existing sets need a one-time source-pinned aggregate backfill; no weekly downloads.
