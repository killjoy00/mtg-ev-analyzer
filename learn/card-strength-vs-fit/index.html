When to take the card that fits your deck

Sometimes the strongest card in the pack isn't the best pick for your deck. Here's how to tell when that matters.

Advertisement

Which cards are strongest on their own?

First, rank the strongest cards without looking at the earlier picks. Then look at the pool. If that changes your first choice, what changed your mind?

This is useful when one card feels obvious because it matches your colors. That may be enough, especially later in a draft. But check how much stronger the other options are before dismissing them.

What does this pool need?

“It fits my deck” is too vague to teach you much. Be specific. Are you short on early plays? Does your mana make the stronger card hard to cast? Do you already have the payoffs that make a synergy card great? Is one kind of card easy to find later while the other is rare?

Write down the reason before the reveal. That gives you something specific to reconsider when you see the trophy pick and model support.

How much weaker is the card you're taking?

If two cards are close, taking the one that fits your deck can make sense. But passing a much stronger card just because you're already in a color needs a better reason.

A useful routine: rank your top three cards, name one or two things the pool needs, then make your pick. After the reveal, ask whether you’d change your choice and why.

Would you still make the same pick?

If the trophy drafter or model prefers a different card, look at where you disagree. Do you rate the cards differently on their own, or do you disagree about what this pool needs?

You might have overlooked a strong card, put too much weight on the mana curve, or spotted something the model missed. Neither the model nor a trophy finish proves that a pick was right.

Keep your original reasoning in view.

Once you know what the drafter took, it’s easy to find reasons for that choice. Compare those reasons with what you thought before the reveal. If you’ve changed your mind, what did you miss? If you haven’t, what still makes your card the better pick for you?
