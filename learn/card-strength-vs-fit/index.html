When to take the card that fits your deck

Sometimes the strongest card in the pack isn't the best pick for your deck. Here's how to tell when that matters.

Advertisement

Start with the pick you'd make with no pool.

Before you use your pool to explain a pick, figure out which cards in the pack are strongest on their own. You're not pretending your deck doesn't matter. You're giving yourself a starting point. If Card A is clearly better than Card B before you look at your pool, you should need a real reason to flip them.

This helps most when a pick feels obvious because one card is in your colors. Being in your colors matters, but it doesn't prove the weaker card is now the better pick.

Say exactly why your deck changes the pick.

“It fits my deck” is too vague to teach you much. Be specific. Are you short on early plays? Does your mana make the stronger card hard to cast? Do you already have the payoffs that make a synergy card great? Is one kind of card easy to find later while the other is rare?

The more specific the reason, the easier it is to check after the reveal. You can disagree with the result and still learn whether your reason held up.

How much weaker is the card you're taking?

If two cards are close, taking the one that fits your deck can make sense. But passing a much stronger card just because you're already in a color needs a better reason.

A routine that helps: rank the best three cards on strength alone, write down the one or two things about your deck that matter, make your final ranking, then reveal. Check whether you moved the pick in the right direction, and whether you moved it too much or too little.

Use the reveal to check your reasoning, not to memorize answers.

When the trophy pick or the model disagrees with you, ask two separate questions. Did you judge the cards' strength correctly? And did you read your deck correctly? Those are different skills, and lumping them into one “right or wrong” makes both harder to improve.

If you had the strength right but leaned too hard on your deck, that's a different lesson from missing the best card in the pack.

Explain your deck before the reveal, not after.

Once you see the answer, almost any pool can be turned into a story that supports it. The trick is to tell the story first. Say what your deck needs, how much it matters, and which card it moves. Then let the reveal test the reasoning you actually used.
