First-pick discipline: commit before the reveal

Pick your top three before the reveal.

Before you see what the trophy drafter took, rank the three cards you'd seriously consider. Don't just identify the bomb rare and the best removal spell. Decide which one you'd actually take and how close you think the choice is.

How close do you think it is?

You can like one card more than another and still think it's a close pick. If the model sees a huge difference where you saw a toss-up, that's worth looking into. The reverse is true too.

What made you choose that card?

When you disagree with the drafter or model, look at why. Did you value the card’s strength more highly? Prefer a card that fits more decks? Take a synergy piece before you had the cards to support it? Miss a demanding mana cost? You might change your mind, or you might still prefer your pick. Either way, knowing why helps when a similar choice comes up.

A useful routine: rank three, state your confidence, reveal, then write one sentence explaining the largest difference between your ranking and the model.

Use repetition without memorizing.

If you recognize a pack you’ve already played, you may remember the trophy pick. That makes it harder to judge what you would have taken on your own. Move on to a pack you don’t recognize, or come back later. Practice is more useful when you have to think through the choice.
