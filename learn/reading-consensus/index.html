Limited study

How to read consensus without treating it as truth

Use the shape of model support to distinguish strong signals from legitimately close Limited decisions.

Advertisement

The gap matters as much as the winner.

Don't just look at which card the model ranks first. Look at how close the next card is. A near tie means there may be more than one good pick. A big gap tells you the model has a clear favorite.

Three kinds of disagreement

Sometimes you and the model disagree about which cards are even worth considering. Sometimes you agree on the good cards but rank them differently. And sometimes you just disagree about how close the pick is.

Those disagreements tell you different things. If you overlooked a strong card, that’s worth reviewing. If you and the model prefer different cards among the same top options, it may have been a close call. And if you thought a pick was obvious but the model was split, look at what made it close.

Model support isn't win rate.

A card with 55% support doesn't mean it wins 55% of games. It just tells you how the model rates that card compared with the other choices in the pack.

Think of the model as another opinion, not the final answer. Sometimes the most useful picks are the ones where you disagree with it.

Look for patterns across sessions.

One disagreement can be noise. Ten similar disagreements can be a tendency. If your misses repeatedly involve multicolor cards, expensive interaction, narrow synergy pieces, or flexible lands, that pattern is more useful than any single grade.
