When staying open is worth it

It's good to stay open early, but how much card quality are you willing to give up for it?

Advertisement

Staying open isn't free.

Everyone says they want to stay open early. That's fine, but it isn't free. Take a flexible card over a much stronger one and you're paying real card quality for options you might never use. Sometimes that's the right trade. The question is how much you're giving up and what you expect to learn from it.

Some cards keep you more open than others.

A strong one-color card keeps more doors open than an equally strong gold card. A colorless card is even easier to fit in, but that doesn't make it the best pick on its own. Flexibility is one thing to weigh. It doesn't replace power.

Those earlier picks aren't yours.

Pack One shows what the trophy drafter had already taken. Your earlier picks don't change that pool, because each pick comes from a different draft. Use those cards to understand what the drafter was deciding, not as the start of a deck you're building.

A quick test: if you're taking the more flexible card, say what you're keeping open and what you're giving up. If you can't name either one, “staying open” may just be a story you're telling yourself after the fact.

Commit when the pack tells you to.

Good drafting isn't staying as flexible as possible. It's staying flexible enough to react, then committing when the signal is real. When you review your misses, look for both mistakes: committing too early, and refusing to commit when the payoff was already there.
