Staying open is not the same as avoiding commitment

It's good to stay open early, but how much card quality are you willing to give up for it?

Advertisement

How much are you giving up to stay open?

Taking a weaker card because it leaves more colors open can be reasonable. But how much weaker is it? If you pass a much stronger card, be clear about why you want to wait and what later picks might tell you.

Some cards leave more colors open.

A strong monocolor card can fit more decks than a similarly strong gold card. A colorless card may be easier to use still. That flexibility matters, but so does what the card does once it’s in your deck. Compare both before deciding.

Those earlier picks aren't yours.

Pack One shows what the trophy drafter had already taken. Your choices in earlier questions don't change that pool, because each question is from a different draft. Use those cards to understand what they were deciding, not as the start of a deck you're building.

A practical test: if you choose the more flexible card, name the colors it leaves open and the stronger card you’re passing. Then decide whether that trade is worth it. Saying “I’m staying open” doesn’t tell you much on its own.

When is it time to commit to a color?

Look at the pack and what the drafter has already taken. Is there a strong reason to take a card in one color, or are several colors still plausible? When you review your picks, consider both possibilities: you may have committed too early, or passed a strong card because you wanted to keep waiting.
